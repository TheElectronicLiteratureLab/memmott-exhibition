Talan Memmott

Talan Memmott (born 1964 in San Francisco, California) is an American digital writer, artist, theorist, and educator with a career spanning nearly three decades at the intersection of electronic literature, digital poetics, and new media art. His work consistently probes the relationships between language, code, design, and digital culture, contributing significantly to the development of born-digital artistic and literary practice.

Memmott's path into digital art was unconventional, shaped in part by early experience performing in a punk band and an ongoing engagement with performance, music, and visual art (video, assemblage), before finding its focus in hypermedia writing and networked expression. He gained wide recognition in 1999 with Lexia to Perplexia, a work of codework and digital poetry that combines dense theoretical language, interface design, and HTML scripting to explore user identity, internet attachment, and networked experience. The piece earned him a nomination for the Electronic Literature Award in 2001 and is frequently cited in scholarship on early electronic literature.

Around the same period, Memmott founded and served as Creative Director of BeeHive, a hypermedia literary journal that sought to integrate thoughtful web design with literary practice at a time when most online publications had yet to explore the formal possibilities of the medium.

He holds an MFA in Literary Arts/Electronic Writing from Brown University (2004) and a PhD in Interaction Design (with a concentration in Digital Rhetoric and Poetics) from Malmö University, Sweden (2012). His doctoral dissertation, “Digital Rhetoric and Poetics: Signifying Strategies in Electronic Literature,” argues that traditional rhetorical and poetic frameworks are ill-equipped to address the multi-modal nature of electronic texts, and proposes alternative critical approaches suited to digital signification.

Memmott has held faculty and research positions at the University of California, Santa Cruz; California State University, Monterey Bay; the University of Colorado Boulder; the Georgia Institute of Technology; and Blekinge Institute of Technology (Sweden). He currently serves as Associate Professor at Winona State University, where he founded the Creative Digital Media program.

His editorial work includes co-editing the Electronic Literature Collection, Volume 2 (with Laura Borràs, Rita Raley, and Brian Stefans) and the ELMCIP Anthology of European Electronic Literature, both widely used scholarly resources in the field. He has served on the Board of Directors for the Electronic Literature Organization and on editorial boards of several peer-reviewed journals.

His current research interests include meme culture, digital performance, glitch aesthetics, disability aesthetics, generative AI, and the rhetoric of networked environments.

In 2021, he received the Maverick Award from the Electronic Literature Organization in recognition of his contributions to the field. He is the author of more than 40 creative works and maintains an active scholarly presence, with citations across a broad range of electronic literature and digital media scholarship.
